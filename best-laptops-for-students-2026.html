What Should Students Look for in a Laptop?

Your university major and daily workload should determine the laptop you buy. A student writing essays has different needs from someone studying engineering, programming or video production.

  • Processor: Choose a processor appropriate for your workload. Basic study tasks require less power than demanding technical or creative applications.
  • RAM: More memory can help when you keep many browser tabs and applications open at the same time.
  • Storage: SSD storage provides fast everyday performance. Consider how many documents, applications, photos and videos you expect to store.
  • Battery life: Students often work away from power outlets, making battery endurance an important practical consideration.
  • Portability: If you carry the laptop to university every day, size and weight matter.
  • Operating system: Check whether your university or course requires Windows, macOS or particular software.
  • Keyboard and display: Students may spend hours reading and writing, so a comfortable keyboard and good-quality display can make a real difference.
  • Connectivity: Check the ports you need for USB devices, external displays, storage and other accessories.
